What to look for

Drag the width. In A the panel groups hold even shares of the row while the panels do not, so the distance between a group and the panel it drives changes with every width and is never zero. In B and C each group is pinned to its panel's column, so the first button's left edge and the panel title's left edge are the same number at every width. Turn on panel guides to see the columns the numbers are talking about.

The tower is the case that settles it. Its panels are 1:2:2 by ratio, so there is no even share of anything to be approximately right about, and the strip in A points at the gaps between panels rather than at panels.

D is the one to read for rhythm rather than for alignment. Scroll the four arrangements with the same figure selected and watch where the top of the chart falls: in A, B and C it moves as the apparatus above it grows, and in D it does not. The legend sits close under the drawing it reads, the panel groups a clear step below it, and the figure wide controls in a box centered under the whole thing, which is what says they govern all of it.

D is also the only arrangement drawing short labels at one width. Every toggle has a floor of about what log x needs, so the row reads as a row and a cycling button stops changing size as it moves through its modes. Hover any of them: a shortened label's tooltip opens by spelling the short form out, so ret prd answers "Return period" before it explains anything.

Muted buttons swaps the pressed toggle from the house red to the same hue at a third of the saturation. The comparison worth making is with the sub-tab row, which is red and a few pixels away: in the saturated reading a pressed chart toggle is the loudest thing in the block and reads as another tab, and in the muted one the chart is.

Below WIDE_PX, 720, the panels stack one per row and A switches to its stacked arrangement, which is labeled and correct. B and C keep the same rule they use everywhere: sit at the column, whatever the column is. Note where the stacked fallback fires: A turns over at a fixed 720, while B and C can turn over when a button stops fitting its column, which is a measurement rather than a guess.
